## 1. Research objective

Produce a second, independent generation of brand candidates for the app (working name
"Prayer Lock"; repository and identifiers unchanged). The app's loop is
**distraction → pause → prayer / mantra / dua / reflection → intentional unlock**. Christianity,
Hinduism and Islam are first-class audiences, and other traditions may follow. The brand must
not feel owned by any one tradition and must have room to grow into scripture, an AI assistant,
calendars, journaling, habits and journeys.

The output is **Tier A** (exactly 7 names, alphabetical, unranked) and **Tier B** (exactly 5
reserves, each with a named unresolved issue). **No final brand is chosen.**

## 2. Lessons from Phase 4

Each docs/17 survivor had a weakness:

| Survivor | Weakness found (docs/17) |
|---|---|
| Dawnstill | reads as a person's name ("Dawn Still"); "Dawn" prefix crowded |
| Lampward | lampward.com is a fortune-stick divination site |
| Quietwell | quietwell.com is a spiritual-wellness organisation; "-well" reads as generic wellness |
| Turnward | exact-name software company holds the `.com`; TURNWORD sound-alike; conversion connotation |
| Waystill | `.com` and Instagram held by retailers; STILLWAY/Waysil near marks; subtle Christian reading |

Patterns this phase was designed around:

1. **Dictionary words are already taken in our category.** Phase 4 lost Stillness, Heed,
   Sojourn and others to same-category apps. Phase 5 confirmed this: 30 of 68 names that reached
   the app-store screen were eliminated there (§6–§7).
2. **The exact `.com` is usually held**, so a coined compound has better domain odds (§10).
3. **Conflicts surface late.** Soulward's `.app` launched 4 days before the Phase 4 search, so
   every name must be re-screened immediately before any decision (§16).
4. **Spiritual-adjacent businesses are a recurring hazard.** Phase 4 found divination
   (lampward.com) and spiritual wellness (Quiet Well Project). Phase 5 found a pending tarot
   and astrology software mark (GLOAMING), a Christian ministry (Uplook) and a spiritual
   organisation (Springhouse).
5. **Faith-lean readings come from vocabulary** ("turning", "be still"), so this phase favours
   concrete, shared practices over doctrinal words (§3).

## 3. Naming strategy

| Dir | Concept | What we looked for | Deliberately avoided |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Intentional attention | choosing and returning attention; remembering what matters | "Focus", "Mindful", "Attention", "Presence" |
| **B** | The moment before action | threshold, doorway, the breath before acting, musical holds and rests | "Pause", "Stillness", "Threshold" |
| **C** | Return | coming home, inward turning — without saying "return" | "Return", "Revert", any "-ward" |
| **D** | Devotion without exclusivity | commitment and practice shared by all three traditions | "Devotion", "Prayer", "Bhakti", "Ikhlas", "Covenant" |
| **E** | Sacred space | a small, non-architectural sacred space | Sanctuary, Temple, Chapel, Mosque, Church, Shrine |
| **F** | Original coinages | 2–4 syllables, spellable after one hearing, explainable construction | "-ora", "-ly", "-ify", fake Sanskrit/Arabic/Latin meanings |

**Construction rule for F:** every coined name is a transparent English compound. Its parts
and intended image are stated in §4. No invented name is given a meaning in any other
language.

**Cultural findings that shaped generation** — general knowledge, all [I], and all need expert
review:

- **"Return" vocabulary carries conversion meanings.** Many converts to Islam call themselves
  "reverts". In India, *ghar wapsi* ("homecoming") is a politically charged reconversion
  slogan. So direction C avoided literal "return" or "home-coming" words in Hindi or English.
- **Some common devotional symbols are not shared by all three traditions:**
  - bells: not part of Islamic worship
  - candles and fire: not used in Islamic worship, where fire is also hell imagery
  - music: its permissibility is debated among Muslims
  - dogs: ritually impure in many Muslim schools
  - kneeling: reads as Christian in English
  Names built on these were excluded or flagged.
- **Shared practices that do span all three traditions**, used as building blocks:
  - **prayer beads:** *japa mala*; *tasbih*/*misbaha*; rosary and prayer rope
  - **joined or folded hands:** *anjali*/namaste; folded hands; hands folded in *qiyam*
  - **breath-paced repetition:** *japa*; breath-linked *dhikr*; the Jesus Prayer
  - **words at the threshold:** *bismillah* on entering; house blessings; auspicious threshold
    markings
- **False friends found while generating:** *jaali* (lattice) also means "counterfeit" in
  Hindi. "Heron" recalls the Hindi idiom *bagula bhagat* (a heron-devotee, i.e. a religious
  hypocrite). "Ajar" matches the Arabic *ajr* (spiritual reward) and is already used by Islamic
  apps (§7).

## 4. 100-candidate generation

**Round 1 (100 names)** was generated across directions A–F. Two short follow-up rounds were
added after the screens showed a pattern. **Round 2 (15)**: most real words died on app-store
and web conflicts, while coined compounds built on shared practices (beads, hands) came through
clean, so round 2 explored that vein. **Round 3 (7)**: the leading group was dominated by
"bead" names, so round 3 tried other concepts (threshold, cupped hands, home). Total:
**122 names, none of which was a candidate in docs/14–17 or among Phase 4's 103** (checked by
script). Five early drafts (Kairos, Interlude, Vigil, Selah, Noor) had been docs/14 candidates
and were dropped before screening. Four words that appear in those docs only as competitor
names or vocabulary (Covenant, Rite, Devout, Bhakti) are in the pool and fail the first
filter. The extra 22 names are a deliberate overshoot, reported openly.

## 17. Unresolved questions

1. Should the brand be built on **one shared practice** (beads; hands; threshold/breath), or
   stay abstract (Inwend)? Beads are the most practice-rich image but read as Catholic to some
   Protestants and are a generic category word.
2. Is a **partial Islamic fit** acceptable for the hand-gesture names (folded vs raised
   palms)?
3. **Listening tests needed**: Dewbead (UK and India), Inwend, Onebead vs "one beat",
   Doorbreath vs "breathe".
4. **Native-speaker review** in Hindi, Urdu, Arabic, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Bengali and
   Marathi for all Tier A names.
5. **Legal**: registrability of two-word everyday compounds (Doorbreath, Handfold); relatedness
   of HANDFOLD (cl. 25) and of the Indian pharma and medical sound-alikes.
6. **Domains**: whether buying a held `.com` (Onebead, Palmfold, Handfold) is worth it versus
   a brand on `.app`. This is an owner purchase decision; nothing was bought.
7. App Store Connect name reservation, TikTok/Facebook handles, and Google Play package
   namespace — not checked.
8. The descriptor/subtitle approach from docs/14–17 ("Prayer, Mantra & Dua" plus a screen-time
   subtitle) still applies. Lengths should be re-counted for the chosen name.
